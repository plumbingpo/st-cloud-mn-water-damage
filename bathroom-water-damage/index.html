Bathroom Water Damage Help in St. Cloud

A bathroom is designed to handle water on its surfaces, never below them. Every flush and every shower lets a small amount of water by after the caulk cracks, the grout wears down or the seal at the toilet fails. That water gathers where you cannot see it, in the subfloor and inside the tiled wall. We help St. Cloud homeowners by tracing the leak path, checking its reach with meters, and drying or removing whatever got wet.

Slow leaks are the norm here, which means what you find has typically been there longer, and gone further, than it seems. The tile can look perfectly fine while the subfloor beneath the toilet has gone soft clear through. Our crew maps the wet area using meters and a thermal camera so nothing extra gets torn out, and only spots that cannot dry where they are get opened.

Warning Signs

  • Hours after anyone showered, the grout is still dark with moisture
  • Floor that feels spongy by the vanity or next to the tub
  • Musty odor in the bathroom that cleaning does not get rid of
  • The ceiling beneath the bathroom shows a brownish ring or has started to droop
  • The bottom panel inside the vanity has swelled, stained or bowed
  • Peeling paint or swollen baseboard on the wall next to the shower
How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Find the Leak

We test the tub, shower, toilet and supply lines one at a time, and use moisture meters and thermal imaging to see where water has traveled under tile and inside walls.

2

Remove What Is Ruined

Loose tile, rotted subfloor and soft wall board come out. Where the toilet leaked, we treat the area as contaminated and remove porous material that cannot be cleaned.

3

Dry the Cavities

The subfloor, the framing in the walls and the space above the lower ceiling are dried with air movers and dehumidifiers. Before anything gets closed up, antimicrobial is applied and dry readings are confirmed.

4

Rebuild and Reseal

After replacing backer board and subfloor, we can arrange tile work, a new seal at the toilet, fresh caulking and ceiling repair, which closes the leak path for real.

The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

First days

Water Slips Through

Each shower pushes a little water through the failed caulk or grout. Nothing shows yet, but the subfloor edge and the board behind the tile are absorbing it.

The first several weeks

The Subfloor Never Dries

Showers come too often for the wood beneath the tile to ever dry. Mold starts on the underside of the floor and inside the wall cavity.

One to three months

Signs Start to Appear

Grout darkens, tiles loosen, and the caulk line turns black. If there is a room below, a stain appears on its ceiling and slowly widens.

Six months and beyond

The Structure Suffers

Joists can begin to rot, and the subfloor near the toilet or tub becomes soft. Drying alone no longer fixes it, because carpentry and new tile are now part of the repair.

Hidden Risks

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

The Room Below

Before dripping, water runs along joists and pipes, which can put the downstairs stain several feet away from the real leak. Wiring and light fixtures may lie along that route.

Leaks Inside the Vanity

Behind the towels and bottles kept under the sink, a drain fitting or supply valve can drip for months. Water swells the cabinet bottom, then passes into the back wall and the subfloor.

Rot Under the Toilet

When the wax ring gives out, a bit of water escapes every time the toilet is flushed. It is category 3 water, and the wood surrounding the flange decays until the toilet wobbles.

Mold in the Tiled Wall

Neither grout nor tile is a waterproof barrier without help. Mold takes hold on the wall board once that hidden layer remains wet, and no scrubbing can get to it.
