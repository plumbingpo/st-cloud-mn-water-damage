A sewage backup is one of the most hazardous situations a property owner can face. Anyone who comes in contact with contaminated material is exposed to parasites, bacteria and viruses, among them giardia, E. coli, salmonella and hepatitis, all carried in raw sewage and all dangerous. When it happens in St. Cloud, St. Cloud Restoration is available 24/7 with technicians trained for this work, proper protective gear and disinfectants registered with the EPA, everything needed for a safe decontamination.

The industry classifies sewage as black water, or Category 3, which is the most dangerous and most contaminated type of water damage there is. Because of it, cleanup follows the toughest rules in the trade, which means full protective gear, barriers to contain the area, tearing out any porous material that met sewage and disinfecting all that is left. For Category 3 water damage the reference is IICRC S500, and our crews follow it to bring the building back to safe occupancy.

Here is what the work covers: standing sewage is pumped out, contaminated water is extracted from the structure, porous materials the sewage touched (drywall, insulation, carpet and padding) are torn out and disposed of properly, hard surfaces are disinfected thoroughly, antimicrobials are applied, and the structure is dried so mold does not follow. We also deodorize the affected area with industrial-grade hydroxyl generators and thermal fogging equipment to eliminate lingering sewage odors.

Sewage Cleanup Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Find your sewer cleanout now and check its condition, so it can be reached in an emergency
  • Every 2 to 3 years, get a sewer camera run through the line to spot developing issues
  • Hair and other debris are easy to stop with simple strainers on tub, shower and sink drains
  • Clear tree roots near sewer lines, which can infiltrate and block the pipes
  • Route sump pump and gutter water away from the sewer line, which can back up when storm flow overloads it
  • Protect against municipal sewer backups by having a backwater valve fitted where your line connects

Signs It Is Time for Sewage Cleanup

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Dark residue from sewage left on the basement floor or ringing a drain
  • More than one drain in the house overflowing or backing up together
  • People in the household getting stomach or intestinal symptoms with no clear cause
  • Toilet water with waste in it soaking into carpet or running across the floor
  • Strong sewage or rotten egg odors coming from drains or lower levels
  • Damp patches on a wall or floor that also smell foul
  • Sewage or dark water backing up through floor drains, toilets or tubs

What a Professional Brings to Sewage Cleanup

No homeowner should try to clean up sewage personally. The biological hazards in raw sewage require specialized training, proper personal protective equipment (Tyvek suits, respirators, protective gloves and boots) and EPA-registered disinfection products that are not available to consumers. Exposure to sewage pathogens can cause serious illness, including gastrointestinal disease, hepatitis, respiratory infections and skin infections.

Disposal matters as well, since everything the sewage contaminated has to be handled as biohazard waste. Putting carpet, insulation or drywall soaked with sewage out with household trash breaks the law and endangers sanitation workers and your community. Licensed biohazard disposal facilities take this waste from certified restoration firms, which keep the required protocols in place.
