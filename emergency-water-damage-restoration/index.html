How to Prevent Emergency Water Damage Restoration Problems

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • A sump pump should be tried out before storms arrive, ideally with a backup battery installed.
  • Pipes running through unheated areas need insulation to keep them from freezing and bursting.
  • Important paperwork and valuables belong above floor level, most of all on the lowest story.
  • Keep the number of an emergency restoration company saved in your phone before you need it.
  • Replace old supply hoses on washers, toilets and faucets before they burst.
  • Being able to find the electrical panel quickly means wet areas can be de-energized without risk.

Why Hire a Professional for Emergency Water Damage Restoration?

Mopping the puddle in front of you is rarely the real challenge during an emergency. It is knowing where the rest of the water went. Within minutes it slips under baseboards, inside wall cavities and below the floor. With a thermal imaging camera and moisture meters we locate that hidden water and treat it the same night, before it turns up weeks later as an odor or a stain.

Truck-mounted extractors, commercial-grade air movers and dehumidifiers matched to the room come with the crew, and no household keeps those around. The crew is trained to stay safe near contaminated water and wet wiring, and from hour one it records the loss in photos and moisture readings. Those records are useful if you file an insurance claim. The same team that takes the emergency call sees the work through until your home is dry.

Signs You Need Emergency Water Damage Restoration in St. Cloud

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Floor-level power strips, plugged-in appliances or the breaker panel are now wet.
  • You have no way to locate or get to the shutoff while water continues to pour.
  • The tank on your water heater gave out and its contents are spreading through the room.
  • The water is from a sewer backup or looks dark and smells bad.
  • Water is coming through a ceiling, light fixture or electrical outlet.
  • Standing water is spreading across floors toward other rooms or down the stairs.
  • You came home to water and do not know how long it ran.
